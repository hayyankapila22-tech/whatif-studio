## Script
Why does your foot fall *asleep*? / You stand up, and it's totally *numb!* Then come the *pins and needles*. / Okay! Your
foot talks to your brain through long *nerves*, like cables. / Sit on it, or cross your legs too long, and you *squish* that
nerve. / Squished, it gets less *blood* too, so the messages go *quiet*. / That's the *numb* part. / Now you *move*. The
pressure's off, and the nerve wakes up *firing* all at once! / That's the *tingle!* And it's gone in a *minute or two*. /
The trick: *switch* positions and stand up every so *often*. / But tingling out of *nowhere*, or that won't go *away*? See a
doctor. / So,

## Facts (fact-checked 2026-11-22)
- MSD Manual "When the Foot Is Asleep": pressure on the nerve; pressure interferes with the nerve's blood supply → pins and needles; moving restores it.
- The Conversation (2021) "What happens when your foot falls asleep?": nerves squished by how you sit; pins and needles as they "wake up"; lasts a minute or two; switch position, don't cross legs too long, stand up every so often.
- Cleveland Clinic (2023) "Should you worry about pins and needles?": usually harmless, gone after a couple of minutes; see a doctor if it comes suddenly without a clear cause or is constant.
