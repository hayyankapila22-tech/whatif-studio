## Script

air can be as thin as on a *2,400 metre* mountain. / The air outside your ear gets *thinner*, so the air inside pushes your

*tube* from your ear to your throat. / When it opens, air rushes through, and... *pop!* / Landing is usually *worse*, so
stay *awake*, and *yawn*, *swallow* or chew *gum*. / So,

## Facts (fact-checked 2026-11-21)
- Eustachian tube links middle ear to the back of the nose/throat and evens out pressure; fast changes → blocked, muffled
  feeling — Mayo Clinic "Airplane ear".
- Usually worse on descent/landing; air pushed up the tube makes the ears pop; stay awake for the descent — patient.info.
- Yawn, swallow, chew gum; don't sleep during takeoff/landing — Mayo Clinic.
- Cabin pressure altitude ≤ 8,000 ft (~2,400 m) in normal operation — FAA 14 CFR 25.841.
