## Script (≈ 85 words, 27.3 s, HIW_SPEED 0.96)

Sliding off the seat, your clothes *rub*, and you pick up extra *charge*. *Thousands* of volts, just waiting.
Then your finger gets close to *metal*… and it all jumps across in one *spark*.
*Trolleys* do it too: their rubber wheels *trap* the charge. And dry winter air makes it *worse*.

## Facts (fact-checked 2026-11-19)
- Rubbing moves electrons; charge builds on you and leaves as a shock when you touch something — Library of Congress, Everyday Mysteries.
- Static shocks are roughly 4,000–35,000 V but carry almost no current: they hurt, they aren't dangerous; cold dry air = more shocks — AccuWeather.
- Sliding across the car seat charges you; keep a hand on the metal frame as you slide out; or touch the car with your key first — Jalopnik.
- Trolley tyres and shop floors are highly insulating, so charge can't leak away; discharges last a few hundred nanoseconds — J. Smallwood, Electrostatic Solutions.
- Car body is near ground; the charge discharges through the first contact (your hand) — YourMechanic.
